来源：微信。ICONIQ 的 Frontier 对谈里，Physical Intelligence 联创、伯克利教授 Sergey Levine 谈到：能不能训练一个模型，让它成为不同形态机器人共享的大脑，从各种物理交互里继续学。原视频见 [ICONIQ](https://www.iconiq.com/videos/physical-intelligence-co-founder-sergey-levine-on-building-a-brain-for-robots)。

**人觉得容易的动作，机器反而最难。** 象棋在上世纪九十年代就能击败世界冠军，围棋又过了约二十年，连贯的自然语言再晚几年。顺序和直觉相反。感知和运动控制对人轻松，是因为不会这些就活不下来，熟练几乎是基因里自带的本能，所以很难体会背后的工程有多难。这是莫拉维克悖论。Marvin Minsky 曾觉得计算机视觉可以作为一个暑假的本科课题，做完再去研究规划；视觉到现在仍没有被解决。Levine 再推进一步：人与计算机的交互也是通过身体完成的。一台机器若能控制自己的身体、做完人能做的物理活动，这个问题其实把其他 AI 问题都包了进去。

**通用预训练改变的是新任务还要多少数据。** 只给某一个控制器、某一项家务收集海量数据，铺不开。这和 2010 年代只做机器翻译的系统一样：专门收集英、法、日等成对语料，后来被通用语言模型盖掉，因为通用模型在更底层理解语言，翻译只是其中一种能力。机器人也会走这条路。PI 的同事 Chelsea Finn 用 Benjie Holson 写的“机器人奥运会”做了测试。那不是跑马拉松或打乒乓球，而是翻袜子、用沾了洗洁精的海绵洗平底锅这类又乱又细的家务。每项给四个小时。超过 90% 做成了，数据最多的也不到八小时；做不成的多半是硬件限制，比如夹爪太宽，伸不进衬衫袖子。同一套算法、同一个模型设计，去掉通用模型初始化，一项都没成功。

**模型变强之后，最好的数据组合会变。** 起步仍是人采集的优质遥操作示范，先把模型训起来。能力够了，就可以用语言辅导。空气炸锅那个例子里，机器人能摸索着使用，但比正常慢很多；给出分步骤指令（抓住把手、拉开、再做下一步）之后，它能利用已经有的物理理解，把口头指导吸收进去。再往后才用得上强化学习。机器人可以自己在真实世界里试，不必每次都等人遥操作。这些试出来的过程就是机器人自主采集的数据：有做对的，也有做砸的，里面没有一份“请照着模仿”的标准动作。强化学习靠任务结果来学，这些数据才变成训练信号。做砸的那次不会被当成正确动作去模仿：结果记成失败，导致失败的动作以后更少被选中；做成的则相反。记录是真实的，错的是这次尝试的结果。人类活动视频和遥操作也不会消失。所以不是选定一种数据就买到底。理想组合跟着能力走，是一个移动靶。真实部署里的自主试错，会变成新的学习来源。

**亲自部署，是为了做出可复用的智能层。** 模型要在真实工作里做成事，而不是停在实验室演示，团队就得自己把部署走通，把问题的各个方面看清楚。但实体工作的范围太广，没有一个团队能同时懂手术、挖掘机，以及用机器人拖拉机给奶牛送饲料。Levine 把这一层看成操作系统。机器人领域大约每十年就会有人再提一次“机器人操作系统”，而且多半失败，因为大家把它理解成一个软件层。操作系统真正的作用是把应用和硬件分开。对人来说，你不会指挥他激活左前臂第 37 号神经元，只需要告诉他做什么，必要时演示一遍。硬件的人做硬件，应用的人做场景，中间那个很难的科学问题用共享的智能层，而不是每家新公司都重做全栈。他用自动驾驶作类比：软件和整车供应链已经分开之后，别人生产大量汽车，并不是写自动驾驶软件的人最操心的事。

**物理智能可能反哺其他 AI。** 这不是在自然语言、视觉旁边再加一个“机器人应用”。视觉、语言、规划、运动控制、强化学习都得合在一起，物理世界的问题更像数字世界问题的超集。人组织周围世界的很多方式，来自我们有身体：计算机按现在这样工作，界面按现在这样设计，是因为我们用身体经验理解世界。机器若能以同样的方式理解物理世界，它面对那些本来为人设计的东西，基础会好得多。

**最重要的进展，可能只是事情自然地运转起来。** 机器人做后空翻会让人惊讶，因为自己不会；它拿起杯子、倒上咖啡、递到手里，人会觉得“这我也会”。所以不一定有一个公众突然惊醒的 ChatGPT 时刻。更可能是能力稳步增长，事情在后台被做好，然后我们不再觉得奇怪。持续看的人，也许会对接下来一年的进展感到意外；如果工作做得好，外人只会觉得本来就该这样。

## English

Source: WeChat. In an ICONIQ Frontier conversation, Physical Intelligence co-founder and UC Berkeley professor Sergey Levine talks about whether one model can become a shared brain for robots of different bodies, and keep learning from physical interaction. The original video is on [ICONIQ](https://www.iconiq.com/videos/physical-intelligence-co-founder-sergey-levine-on-building-a-brain-for-robots).

**Actions that feel easy for people are the hardest for machines.** Chess programs beat the world champion in the 1990s. Go took about twenty more years. Coherent natural language came a few years after that. The order runs against intuition. Perception and motor control feel easy because people would not survive without them. That fluency is close to an instinct carried in our genes, which makes the engineering difficulty hard to feel. This is Moravec's paradox. Marvin Minsky once thought computer vision could be an undergraduate summer project, after which everyone could move on to planning. Vision is still unsolved. Levine pushes the point further: people also interact with computers through their bodies. A machine that could control its own body and do the physical things a person can do would be taking in the rest of the AI problem as well.

**General pretraining changes how much data a new task needs.** Collecting a huge dataset for one controller, or for one household chore, does not scale. It resembles machine-translation systems of the 2010s, which gathered paired text for English, French, Japanese, and other languages, and were later covered by general language models. Those models understand language at a more basic level, and translation is one ability among others. Robots are on the same path. Chelsea Finn, a colleague at PI, tested this on the "robot Olympics" described by Benjie Holson. The events were not a marathon or table tennis. They were messy, fine-grained chores: turning a sock inside out, or washing a pan with a sponge full of dish soap. Each task was given four hours. More than 90% were solved, and even the one that needed the most data took less than eight hours. The failures were mostly hardware limits, such as a gripper too wide to reach into a shirt sleeve. With the same algorithm and the same model design, but without initialization from the general model, none of the tasks succeeded.

**As the model gets stronger, the best mix of data changes.** The starting point is still high-quality teleoperated demonstrations, collected by people, to get the model trained. Once it is capable enough, language coaching works. In the air-fryer example, the robot could muddle through, but much more slowly than normal. Given step-by-step instructions — grab the handle, pull it open, then do the next thing — it could use the physical understanding it already had and take in the spoken guidance. Reinforcement learning becomes usable only later. The robot can try on its own in the real world, without a person teleoperating every attempt. Those attempts are the data it collects autonomously. Some succeed and some fail, and none of them is a standard demonstration that says "copy this." Reinforcement learning trains on the outcome of the task, which is what turns the data into a training signal. A failed attempt is not imitated as the correct action. The result is recorded as failure, and the actions that led there are chosen less often afterward. Successful attempts go the other way. The record is real. What went wrong is the result of that attempt. Video of people doing things, and teleoperation, do not go away. There is no one kind of data to buy and be done with. The right mix moves with capability. Autonomous trial and error in real deployment becomes a new source of learning.

**They deploy the systems themselves in order to build a reusable intelligence layer.** If the model is going to get real work done, and not stop at a lab demo, the team has to carry deployment through and understand each part of the problem. Physical work is too broad for one team to know surgery, excavators, and a robot tractor feeding cows. Levine thinks of this layer as an operating system. About once a decade, robotics tries again to build a robot operating system, and the attempts mostly fail, because people treat it as a software layer. What an operating system does is separate applications from hardware. You do not tell a person to activate neuron 37 in the left forearm. You tell them what the job is, and show them if needed. People who are good at hardware can stay on hardware. People who want to build an application can stay on the application. They do not each have to solve the very hard scientific problem in the middle. They can use a shared intelligence layer. His comparison is self-driving cars. Once software and the vehicle supply chain are already split apart, another company building a lot of cars is not the main worry of the people writing the driving software.

**Physical intelligence may improve the rest of AI in return.** This is not a robotics application added beside natural language and vision. Vision, language, planning, motor control, and reinforcement learning all have to be combined. A problem in the physical world is more like a superset of problems in the digital world. A lot of how people organize the world comes from having bodies. Computers work as they do, and interfaces are designed as they are, because we understand the world through bodily experience. A machine that could understand the physical world through a body, as we do, would have a much better basis for everything that was originally made for people.

**The most important progress may simply be that things run as a matter of course.** A robot doing a backflip is surprising, because we cannot do one. A robot that picks up a cup, pours coffee, and hands it to someone feels ordinary, because we can do that too. There may be no single ChatGPT moment when the public suddenly sees how capable physical AI is. The more likely path is a steady increase in ability: the important milestones get the work done in the background, and then we no longer find them surprising. People who keep watching may be surprised by what shows up over the next year. If the work is done well, everyone else will just feel that things are working, and that this is how it naturally is.
